# Evidence matrix

| Mechanism | Observable implication | Evidence | Alternative explanation | Preliminary assessment |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **P1** Recognition without status equality | Achievement-acknowledging sentences contain co-occurring hierarchy-preserving language (superiority-in-another-dimension, subsidy/external-cause attribution, delegitimizing labels) | jpn_kor: 9/42 achievement items (21.4%), 6 distinct mechanisms, each with a direct quote. kor_chn: 0/31. Cross-case p=0.0082 | Genre effect (adversarial Diet debate vs. controlled press material) is at least as plausible as a theoretical difference | **Supported within jpn_kor; cross-case comparison confounded and not usable as-is** |
| **P2** Competitive threat substitutes for dismissal | R1 (subordinate) share falls and R3 (threat) share rises across time, specifically within the subordinate-or-threat subset | kor_chn: R1/R3 = 12/2 (early) → 4/8 (late), p=0.014. jpn_kor: 9/8 → 3/8, p=0.253 (same direction, not significant) | Recognition adjustment (dismissal fades into peer language, not threat language) -- the two predict different R1-vs-R3 trajectories and the kor_chn data favor competitive recognition | **Supported for kor_chn; directionally consistent but underpowered for jpn_kor** |
| **P3** Sectoral vs. national recognition | Sector-specific (named-industry) recognition differs systematically from general economic recognition | Only 6-8 classified items carry an explicit sector tag; these skew toward R3/R5 | Too few observations to rule out chance | **Inconclusive -- hypothesis-generating only, not tested** |
| **P4** Elite/institutional disagreement | Government-coalition speakers show different recognition framing than opposition speakers | LDP peer share 0.44 (n=16) vs. other-party 0.34 (n=35), p=0.547; chamber split 0.389 vs 0.382 | Historical party/coalition composition changes over the 44-year span in ways this pilot's simplified LDP/other split does not control for | **Null result -- no detectable institutional effect at this sample size** |
| **P5** Triangular status hierarchies | Explicit multi-country comparisons position the rising state relative to a third power, not only the incumbent | 3 strong individually-quoted examples (`jpnkor_0030` historical USA-JPN-KOR parallel; `jpnkor_0132`, `jpnkor_0147` peer-tier groupings with US/Europe/Australia/India) | Co-mention is not the same as an explicit ranking comparison; most country co-mentions in the corpus are procedural (trade-agreement partner lists), not status claims | **Qualitatively supportive; not systematically coded or quantified in this pass** |
| **P6** Attention vs. recognition | Discourse volume and peer-recognition share move independently, not in lockstep | Pearson r = -0.634 across 10 periods (full 10,742-speech corpus as attention measure vs. classified-subset peer share) | NDL digitization coverage is uneven by decade for reasons unrelated to real-world salience, which could itself drive the volume series independent of any attention mechanism | **Directionally supported (volume and recognition do NOT move together, and if anything move oppositely); descriptive only, n=10 periods** |
| **P7** Domain-specific recognition / selective distancing | Economic recognition rises while security/territorial framing stays hierarchical or adversarial | Several R0-coded items in the existing data are specifically Dokdo/Takeshima territorial disputes, coded R0 because they carry no economic-status content at all, occurring across the same years as rising economic peer-share | Framing R0 (dispute items) as evidence of "selective distancing" risks circularity -- these items were never coded on the economic R-scale to begin with, so "domain-specific recognition" cannot be directly demonstrated from this coding scheme without a parallel security/political R-scale | **Not tested -- the existing coding scheme cannot distinguish this from P7's null by construction; flagged as a design gap for future work, not a finding** |
| **P8** Persistence of historical leadership narratives | Incumbent elites invoke former leadership even after conceding present decline, to preserve symbolic distinction | 4 genuine hits, all shipbuilding/tech-specific, clustering in 2020-2024 (`jpnkor_0030`, `jpnkor_0170`, `jpnkor_0177`, `jpnkor_0195`) -- `jpnkor_0177`'s "regain its FORMER glory" is the clearest single example of simultaneous concession and symbolic claim | Simple historical description (narrating the past without an active present-day symbolic claim) -- distinguished here by close reading: each of the 4 hits makes an explicit present-tense normative or comparative claim, not mere narration | **Qualitatively supported; clustering in the most recent period is itself notable and not an artifact of sampling (the stratified sample draws evenly across periods)** |
