# Three strongest new propositions, and final theoretical evaluation

Selected by evidential strength and theoretical distinctiveness from the original recognition-lag
proposition, not by p-value alone (P2's kor_chn result is the only one that clears a conventional
significance threshold; P1 and P8 are included because they are theoretically load-bearing and
grounded in specific, re-verifiable quotations, which this pilot weighs as seriously as a p-value
at n=343).

## Proposition 1: Dismissal converts into competitive threat, not into peer recognition (P2)

**Why theoretically meaningful:** the original recognition-lag framework implicitly treats R1
(subordinate framing) as the thing that *recedes* as R4/R5 (peer framing) rises. This proposition
says something different and more specific: R1 recedes because it is *replaced by* R3 (threat
framing), which is a different and arguably more consequential discourse than peer recognition --
it predicts rivalry and defensive policy response (anti-dumping actions, subsidy complaints,
talent-poaching anxiety -- several of which appear directly in the kor_chn corpus, e.g.
`korchn_0290`'s 2024 concern about Chinese universities recruiting Korean semiconductor
researchers) rather than acceptance.

**Evidence:** supportive and significant for kor_chn (p=0.014); directionally consistent but not
significant for jpn_kor.

**How it differs from the original proposition:** recognition-lag is about *timing* (when does
peer recognition arrive); this proposition is about *substitution* (what arrives in dismissal's
place, which may not be peer recognition at all). The two are not mutually exclusive -- both could
be true for the same case at different industries or time horizons -- but they make different
predictions about what the discourse looks like in the gap between crossover and (if it ever
arrives) full peer recognition.

**What would discriminate further:** classify the remaining ~232 sampled-but-unclassified items in
both annotation-ready files specifically for R1/R3 composition by finer (not just two-way) time
bins, to see whether the kor_chn shift is gradual or itself has a identifiable break point.

**Most valuable human-coded observations:** the 26 kor_chn items used in this specific test
(§7 of the mechanism report) -- small enough for a fast, high-value validation pass.

## Proposition 2: Recognition, when granted, is frequently partial -- qualified by a counter-claim on another dimension (P1)

**Why theoretically meaningful:** this directly operationalizes the "recognition of achievement ≠
recognition as a peer" distinction the original research brief insisted on (Section III.6 of the
overhaul brief) and that the R0-R5 scheme was designed around, but had not yet been tested as its
own hypothesis -- the original scheme separates R2 from R4/R5 by DEGREE of recognition; this
proposition asks whether even the HIGH end (R4/R5) frequently carries an attached qualifier,
which the ordinal scale alone does not capture.

**Evidence:** supported within jpn_kor (9/42, six distinct mechanisms, each independently
quoted and verified against source text). The cross-case comparison is explicitly NOT treated as
evidence here, because of the genre confound (§6 of the mechanism report) -- this proposition
rests on the within-jpn_kor finding alone, which does not depend on the cross-case comparison.

**How it differs from the original proposition:** recognition-lag predicts a single transition
date; this proposition predicts that even post-transition language remains structurally different
from full, unqualified peer recognition -- i.e., there may be no single date at which "recognition
arrives" in an unqualified sense, only a shift in which qualifiers accompany it.

**What would discriminate further:** a same-genre comparison corpus (§6, §8 of the mechanism
report) to test whether the 21% jpn_kor rate is genuinely higher than a Korean equivalent, or
whether it is a property of adversarial parliamentary debate as a genre regardless of country.

**Most valuable human-coded observations:** the 9 positive + 9 matched-negative jpn_kor items
named in the mechanism report §7.

## Proposition 3: Historical-leadership invocation intensifies, not fades, as material decline becomes undeniable (P8)

**Why theoretically meaningful:** a naive reading of "recognition eventually catches up" would
predict that once an incumbent concedes material loss, symbolic claims to past leadership should
fade along with it. The opposite pattern -- symbolic claims clustering exactly when material
concession is most explicit (`jpnkor_0177`'s "regain its FORMER glory" is simultaneously the
clearest concession AND the clearest symbolic claim in the entire sample) -- suggests historical
narrative serves a distinct, ongoing legitimating function independent of the underlying material
facts, which neither the original recognition-lag framework nor P1-P2 directly address.

**Evidence:** qualitative only (4 items, all individually verified), but the clustering (all 4 in
2020-2024, the period with the strongest documented material concession) is itself a pattern, not
cherry-picking -- these are simply every item in the stratified sample that fit the pattern on
systematic re-reading.

**How it differs from the original proposition:** it is not about when recognition arrives, but
about what persists symbolically *after* recognition (of material loss specifically) has already
arrived -- a genuinely distinct empirical object.

**What would discriminate further:** systematic re-reading of the full 5,594-sentence jpn_kor pool
(not just the 175 classified items) for historical-leadership language specifically, to establish
actual prevalence rather than 4 examples found via keyword search on existing notes.

**Most valuable human-coded observations:** all shipbuilding-tagged items across the full relevant
pool (a natural, bounded, high-value subset given the sector's unusually strong material and
discourse documentation in this project).

## Final evaluation (Phase 7)

**Not predetermined; argued from the evidence actually produced in this and the prior session.**

The evidence most favors **(B) Recognition is multidimensional and selectively granted**, with a
secondary, genre-confounded lean toward **(C) convergence generates contestation/differentiation**
specifically for the kor_chn case. It does not favor **(A) recognition lag remains the strongest
explanation** as a sole account:

- The turning-point test itself (prior session) was NOT SUPPORTED under its own pre-specified
  criteria for either case -- the strongest evidence for lag-as-such remains the individually-read
  2008-vs-2020 shipbuilding quotation pair, not a statistically confirmed pattern.
- P1 shows that even where recognition IS granted (R4/R5), it is frequently accompanied by a
  counter-claim on another dimension in the jpn_kor corpus -- consistent with (B), not with a
  single monotonically-rising recognition variable.
- P2 shows, significantly for kor_chn, that dismissal's recession is better explained by
  substitution with threat-framing than by replacement with peer-framing -- consistent with (C)
  for that case specifically.
- P4 found no institutional heterogeneity and P6 found attention and recognition do NOT move
  together -- both are genuine nulls that narrow, rather than expand, the plausible mechanism
  space, which is itself a contribution.

**What would be most interesting to an IPE journal even if recognition-lag ultimately fails:**
Proposition 2 (partial/qualified recognition) directly engages an underspecified claim in the
existing status-recognition literature this project already reviewed (Røren and Beaumont 2019's
point that resource-recognition gaps need case-level explanation, not a single lag statistic --
`docs/literature_review.md`) and gives it a concrete, quote-verified operationalization
(six distinct qualification mechanisms) that the literature review did not find an existing paper
already doing. Proposition 1 (dismissal→threat substitution) is the more publishable SINGLE
RESULT given its significance, but Proposition 2 is the more publishable FRAMEWORK given its
connection to existing theory and its applicability beyond this project's two cases.

**Institutional disagreement (P4) and triangular comparisons (P5) do not, on this pass, suggest
further theoretical alternatives beyond what P1/P2/P8 already surface** -- P4 was a clean null,
and P5's evidence, while real, was too thin (3 examples) to support an independent proposition
distinct from P8's historical-narrative framing (the clearest P5 example, `jpnkor_0030`, IS a
historical-leadership-persistence example).
